Research · Telugu dubbing stack

Why the Telugu comes out sounding American

Your instinct is right, and it has been measured. Western text-to-speech does not fail on Telugu by mispronouncing words — it fails on the consonants and the pitch, which is exactly what a native ear catches and no accuracy metric reports.

Verdict

What to use, and what to test this week

The stack

  1. Voice → Sarvam Bulbul v3. Trained from scratch on Indian speech, not adapted from an English-first model. It beats ElevenLabs v3 and Cartesia Sonic-3 on per-phoneme accent measures, especially on Telugu. ₹30 per 10,000 characters — about ₹3 for a ten-minute talk.
  2. Translation → an LLM for the rewrite, Sarvam Translate as the check. Sarvam Translate beat Gemini Pro, Claude and GPT-5 on Indic translation in 8 of 10 cases and is stronger on formal, structured text; an LLM is still better at the dubbing-specific job of holding tone and hitting a duration budget.
  3. Lip-sync → sync.so lipsync-2 ($0.04–0.05/sec) or HeyGen (~$2/min standard). This is 95% of your cost.

The test, before you spend anything

  1. Sign up for Sarvam (₹100 free credits — no card).
  2. Run scripts/tts_shootout.py. It puts the same Telugu sentences through every provider into a folder with the vendor names stripped from the filenames.
  3. Have a Telugu speaker who is not you score them blind.

Diagnosis

Telugu is the language that breaks English-first models

The PSP benchmark scores Indic TTS on six phonological dimensions instead of one accuracy number. Its central finding is a ranking by how badly systems collapse retroflex consonants — the ones made with the tongue curled back, which English does not have:

In practice: ట ఠ డ ఢ ణ ళ come out as their dental cousins. A Telugu speaker does not think “that consonant was substituted” — they think “that person is not Telugu.” It is the single loudest tell.

The second failure is pitch, and no metric catches it

PSP's prosodic-signature dimension surfaced what the paper calls ElevenLabs Telugu's narrow-pitch-range failure — a pitch range far tighter than a native speaker's, in a different rhythmic class. You hear it as flat and mechanical: every word correct, nobody home.

The important part is the mismatch between metrics. ElevenLabs v3 posts the best word error rate on Hindi (0.006) while failing the prosody dimension on Telugu. WER cannot see this. Any vendor claiming accuracy numbers for Indian languages is measuring the thing that was never broken.

This is a design decision, not a bug

ElevenLabs' voices are built English-first and extended outward, so they carry English phonetics into every other language. The company's own multilingual guidance describes it: teams integrate, then discover their Dutch has an English accent. For a Telugu audience that is not a rough edge — it is the whole impression.

Grammar

Getting Telugu that reads as written by a Telugu speaker

You asked for text an AI can turn into structured, grammatical output. Two tools, and they are good at different halves of the job.

Sarvam Translate — accuracy and formal grammar

In head-to-head evaluation it recorded the highest win rate in 8 of 10 cases against Gemini Pro, Claude and GPT-5, holding above 4.0 on a 5-point expert scale, and it is specifically stronger than general models on formal and structured text. If your priority is correct grammar and case marking, this is the stronger engine.

An LLM — tone, register and length

What a translation model will not do is rewrite a sentence to land in 3.4 seconds while keeping the joke. Dubbing needs meaning over structure, a syllable budget per line, and a decision about which English loanwords to keep — ఆఫీసు, ఫోన్ and టైం are what Telugu speakers actually say, and replacing them with Sanskrit equivalents is the fastest way to sound like a textbook.

Use both: LLM writes the dubbing line, Sarvam Translate renders the same source, and where they disagree materially, a human looks. That disagreement list is short and it is where the errors live.

Lip sync

The layer that costs the money

Lip-sync models are audio-driven, so they are nominally language-agnostic — but they were trained overwhelmingly on English video. Phonemes English lacks, retroflex consonants among them, force the model to invent mouth shapes it has rarely seen. Bilabial accuracy (ప బ మ) needs to clear ~92% before a viewer stops noticing.

Test

The week's work

  1. Get a Sarvam key

    ₹100 in free credits, no card. That is roughly 33,000 characters — about three ten-minute talks, entirely free.

    export SARVAM_API_KEY=...

  2. Run the blind comparison

    Same Telugu sentences through every provider. The sentences are chosen to stress retroflex consonants, vowel length, a question contour and a mid-sentence English loanword — the four things adapted models get wrong.

    python scripts/tts_shootout.py \
    --providers sarvam elevenlabs azure edge_tts \
    --text-file samples/where_is_time_for_yoga_te.json

  3. Score it blind, with someone else's ears

    Output filenames carry no vendor name. Score retroflex, pitch, rhythm and loanwords separately — a system can pass one and fail another, and an averaged "which sounds better" hides exactly that. Open KEY.json only afterwards.

  4. Then, and only then, test one minute of lip-sync

    Take the winning voice, dub sixty seconds, and put it through sync.so or HeyGen. One minute costs about $3 and answers the question that no amount of reading will.

Caveats

What this research cannot tell you

  • I have not heard any of these systems. Every claim above is from published benchmarks and vendor documentation. The blind test exists because your ears and your reviewer's ears are the only evidence that settles it.
  • PSP is a young benchmark on a pilot set of three languages, and it found no system Pareto-optimal across its six dimensions. Treat the ranking as a strong prior, not a verdict.
  • Vendor pricing moves. Sarvam's ₹30/10k is beta pricing. Re-check before committing to volume.
  • Google's Telugu tier needs confirming. Chirp 3 HD's language list is expanding; te-IN may only exist on older tiers. Verify in the console before budgeting.
  • Cloning the speaker's own voice is a separate decision with a consent problem attached, and no bearing on whether the Telugu sounds native. A stock native voice beats a cloned non-native one.
